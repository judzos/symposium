1. Psychology of Memory

Overview — The span of immediate memory and the shape of forgetting

  1. Chunking — Chunking is grouping input into larger familiar units, so that a memory span fixed in the number of units can hold more information.
  2. Forgetting Curve — The forgetting curve is the measured decline of retained learning over time: steep in the first hour, then so slow that complete forgetting would take indefinitely long.

2. Learning and Social Psychology

Overview — How fear, attachment and attitudes are acquired and changed

  1. Conditioned Emotional Response — A conditioned emotional response is an emotion, such as fear, that a neutral stimulus comes to provoke after being paired with one that provokes it innately.
  2. Contact Comfort — Contact comfort is the soothing an infant gets from soft bodily contact, which Harry Harlow found to be the main basis of the infant–mother tie, ahead of feeding.
  3. Cognitive Dissonance — Cognitive dissonance is the tension of holding two cognitions that do not fit together, such as believing X while knowing you have publicly said not-X, and it pushes…

  5. Childhood Wounds and Adult Patterns — Early relationships can supply templates for later expectations about safety, need, conflict, and care.
  6. Avoidant Attachment — The avoidant craves connection as deeply as anyone, then dismantles it the moment it becomes real.

3. Judgment and Decision-Making

Overview — Two modes of thought, heuristics, framing and the value of gains and losses

  1. Dual-Process Theory (System 1 and System 2) — Dual-process theory divides thinking into a fast, automatic, intuitive mode (System 1) and a slow, effortful, deliberate one (System 2) that monitors it, loosely.
  2. Attribute Substitution — Attribute substitution is answering a hard question by silently answering an easier, related one: a judgment is heuristic when a target attribute is assessed through a…
  3. Framing Effect — A framing effect is a change in choice produced by describing the same options differently, in a way the chooser would agree, on reflection, should not matter.
  4. Prospect Theory — Prospect theory is Kahneman and Tversky’s model of risky choice in which outcomes are valued as gains and losses from a reference point, not as final states of wealth.
  5. Loss Aversion — Losing something you already have hurts roughly twice as much as gaining the equivalent feels good — an asymmetry that makes fear-framed messaging outperform aspiration…

4. Behavior Change

Overview — How a new behavior becomes automatic, from first action to identity

  1. Willpower Depletion — Self-control is a finite cognitive resource, a limited daily reserve that depletes with use, which is why discipline always fails as a permanent lifestyle strategy.
  2. Identity Shift — Identity shift updates the self-concept at the level of who you are rather than what you do.
  3. Dark Motivation — Dark motivation is a specific, personal and emotionally charged reason to persist through the effortful start of behavioural change.
  4. Procrastination — Procrastination is an emotion-regulation problem rather than a time-management problem.
  5. Perfectionism — Some perfectionism is a fear response that presents itself as a high standard.

  7. Invisible Growth Phase — During an invisible growth phase, repeated work may change skill, capacity, or process before a chosen outcome metric visibly moves.
  8. Identity Setpoint — The identity setpoint is the brain’s internal model of “who I am,” a baseline that the nervous system enforces through behavior.
  9. Self-Sabotage — Self-sabotage expresses an Identity Setpoint correction: the nervous system returns you to a known identity baseline when success creates an unfamiliar state that feels…
  10. Momentum vs Motivation — Momentum comes from a small start that builds evidence, where motivation is a favourable feeling people wait for.

  13. Limiting Beliefs — A limiting belief is a self- or world-model that narrows attempted action and filters interpretation in a self-confirming direction.
  14. Cognitive Decentering — Cognitive decentering is the stance that a thought can be observed without being treated as the observer’s identity, a fact, or an instruction.
  15. Delayed Gratification — Delayed gratification is the ability to tolerate the gap between effort and reward: to work before results, invest before returns and build before visibility.
  16. Scheduled Commitment — A scheduled commitment moves the decision to act out of the mood in which action must occur.
  17. Compound Effect — Repeated small actions can accumulate while visible results lag.

  25. Self-Efficacy — Self-efficacy is a domain-specific belief about one’s capacity to execute the actions a task requires.

  4. Environmental Design — Environmental design arranges physical and social conditions so a desired behavior receives the strongest cues and lowest friction, in place of treating every action as…
  5. Active Reading — Active reading aims for comprehension and unaided reconstruction.
  6. Decision Fatigue — Decision-making capacity is finite and degrades with use across a day.
  7. Mental Rehearsal — Mental rehearsal simulates a specific future performance in detail before execution.
  8. Attention–Retention Gap — The attention–retention gap measures how much content passes through awareness against how much remains as memory, judgment, action, or a chosen restorative experience.
  9. Skill Acquisition — Practice can optimise performance in the practised setting without producing retention or transfer.
  10. Emotional Literacy — Emotional literacy means noticing a feeling, describing it, asking what information it may contain, and choosing a response without accepting the first interpretation as…
  11. Seiton (Set in Order) – Part of the 5S System — Seiton follows Seiri (Sort) – Part of the 5S System and focuses on organizing what remains so that every item has a designated place.

  15. State Cross-Validation — State cross-validation means generating an idea under one cognitive condition and testing it under the condition that must execute it.

7. Human Nature

Overview — Ego, social comparison, comfort, belief, and predictable behavior

  1. Insecurity — Insecurity is a learned uncertainty about whether a trait, preference, or self-presentation is socially safe.
  2. Universal Selfishness — Universal selfishness is the thesis that even apparently selfless action can provide identity coherence, belonging, pleasure, or social return.
  3. Tribal Psychology — Tribal psychology is the brain’s urgent need for in-group belonging and the psychological systems that evolved to maintain it.
  4. Comfort Zone — In the comfort zone, the brain atrophies, myelin thickens on old pathways, and the capacity to handle challenge degrades.
  5. Comfort Compounding — Comfort follows the mechanics of compound interest in reverse.
  6. Social Comparison — Social comparison is the psychological process of evaluating oneself relative to others.
  7. Hedonic Treadmill — You get what you wanted. The satisfaction lasts three weeks. Then the wanting begins again. This is not a flaw in you — it is how the reward system was designed.
  8. Terror Management Theory — Terror management theory holds that awareness of mortality can motivate investment in cultural worldviews, self-esteem, and groups that confer significance or continuity.
  9. Learned Helplessness — The learning pattern in which repeated uncontrollability reduces later attempts to act, even when action becomes possible.
  10. Apathy & Anhedonia — Apathy is reduced motivation or goal-directed action, and anhedonia is reduced pleasure: distinct but related dimensions.
  11. Derealization — Derealization is a felt distance from the reality of one’s surroundings: a scene seems to occur through glass, colour and sound lose immediacy, and experience is…
  12. Locus of Control — Locus of control describes how strongly a person expects their actions to influence outcomes.
  13. Unstimulated Incubation — Unstimulated incubation is a low-demand interval after or between focused tasks without a new media stream.
  14. Romanticizing Life (Lens Reframing) — People can experience the same objective circumstances as abundance or scarcity depending on their lens.
  15. Temporal Discounting — People value a reward less when it lies further in the future because the future self feels like an abstraction rather than a person.
  16. Digital Addiction — Digital addiction is a structural neurological dependency on technology-mediated dopamine delivery, specifically through smartphones and social media.

8. Social Power & Influence

Overview — Status, persuasion, manipulation, and self-defense

  1. Emotional Coherence — Emotional coherence describes how well a claim fits the story through which a person understands identity, belonging, cause and meaning.
  2. Pattern Over Tells — Pattern over tells is a constraint on interpreting behavior: one gesture has many possible causes, as How To Read People Like A Book sets out.
  3. Dominance vs. Prestige — Dominance and prestige are two status strategies: dominance secures deference through threatened costs, while prestige receives deference for valued competence or…
  4. Dark Triad — The Dark Triad describes three personality profiles that systematically use other people as resources for their own ends: narcissism, Machiavellianism and psychopathy.
  5. Ego Games — Ego games are recurring social negotiations over recognition, expertise, status, validation and uniqueness.
  6. Reading People — Reading people means looking past someone’s presentation to the automatic behavioral patterns that reveal character, internal state, and motivation before the conscious…
  7. Charisma — Charisma is a set of learnable behavioral patterns that trigger specific psychological responses in other people’s nervous systems, rather than magic or an inborn…
  8. Emotional Intelligence — Emotional intelligence is the ability to recognize emotions in yourself and others, process them internally, and choose responses rather than react from raw feeling.
  9. Halo Effect — The halo effect is a cognitive shortcut in which one salient positive trait creates assumptions of other positive traits, typically without evidence.
  10. Status and Dominance Signals — Status inference is fast and partly automatic, and silence, movement, voice, space, disagreement, and response timing are its possible cues.
  11. Calibrated Self-Interest — Calibrated self-interest combines self-protection, pride, ambition, and recognition-seeking with empathy and reciprocity.
  12. Character Under Pressure — Character becomes visible when circumstances change unexpectedly, expectations aren’t met, or something goes differently than planned.
  13. Curiosity Gap — Withholding part of the information creates an open loop the brain feels compelled to close.
  14. Loudness Bias — Loudness bias is the psychological tendency to equate dominant, assertive behavior with intelligence and expertise.
  15. Persuasion Architecture — Persuasion architecture is the set of specific techniques, sequences, and psychological triggers that produce compliance, belief adoption, and behavioral change.
  16. Stimulus–Response Gap — The stimulus–response gap is the trainable interval between an initial reaction and the outward act that follows.
  17. Strategic Adaptation — Strategic adaptation is replacing an inherited method with a better one, putting demonstrated capability near decisions, acting before a slower system can respond, and…
  18. Strategic Understatement — Strategic understatement is the choice not to display all available knowledge, certainty, or emotional intensity in an interaction.
  19. Manipulation Mechanisms — Manipulation pulls on a set of influence levers: narrative, identity, belonging, status, scarcity, loss, and intermittent reinforcement.
  20. Novelty Substitution — Novelty substitution means mistaking a new reward state for evidence of a better relationship.

9. Relationships & Attraction

Overview — Attraction, attachment, heartbreak, and intimacy

  2. Byproduct Signaling — Byproduct signaling is the reading of a visible trait as an output of broader capability, as distinct from the same trait read as the optimized goal.
  3. Presence — Presence is sustained engagement with the immediate activity or person instead of continual switching toward another stimulus.
  4. Von Restorff Effect — The Von Restorff effect is the tendency to remember the one item that differs from its surroundings.
  5. Intermittent Reinforcement — Intermittent reinforcement is the most powerful reward schedule in behavioral science, and the most dangerous when people or systems deploy it against you.
  6. Relationship Medium Substitution — Relationship medium substitution is treating signals from a communication tool as direct measurements of the bond.

  8. Validation Seeking — Validation seeking is a pattern in which external response becomes the main test of worth or quality.

  16. Novelty-Cadence Conditioning — Novelty-cadence conditioning is the learned expectation that stimulation will change after a very short interval.
  17. Scarcity & Desire (Reactance Theory) — The brain uses availability as a proxy for value, treating rare things as more valuable and restricted things as more desirable.
  18. Detachment & Letting Go — You often get what you want once you no longer need it.
  19. Health Signaling Theory — Health Signaling Theory treats many aspects of human physical appearance as cues to underlying physiological health and reproductive fitness.
  20. Heartbreak as Growth — A painful relationship ending can expose repeating patterns and create an occasion for deliberate growth.
  21. Maladaptive Daydreaming — Maladaptive daydreaming is elaborate, recurring fantasy that becomes costly to work, relationships, presence, or action.

11. Self-Improvement Culture

Overview — Productivity advice, and how displaying improvement displaces doing it

  1. Identity as Behavioral Architecture — Identity is a behavioural prior: self-descriptions shape which options feel coherent, what evidence is noticed, and how a lapse is interpreted.
  2. Energy Management — Energy management is a planning principle: available attention and effort constrain how usefully time can be spent.
  3. Goal Announcement Effect — Stating a goal and receiving acknowledgment gives the brain a taste of the completed goal.
  4. Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Motivation — Praise trains the brain to treat being seen as the reward.
  5. Process-Reward Association — A process-reward association forms when beginning and continuing work come to predict cues of value before the delayed outcome arrives.
