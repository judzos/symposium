4. Neuroscience & Biology

Overview — Brain mechanisms behind thought, feeling, action, and recovery

  3. Polyvagal Theory — Polyvagal Theory, Stephen Porges’s framework, describes the autonomic nervous system through three hierarchical states.
  4. HPA Axis & Cortisol — The HPA axis is the body’s primary stress-hormone system and the system most often disrupted in high-achievers who glorify overwork.
  5. Cortisol — Cortisol is a glucocorticoid involved in energy availability, circadian waking, stress response, immune regulation, and many other processes.
  6. Cortisol Awakening Response — The body produces a built-in alertness spike after waking, anchored by early light exposure.
  7. Prefrontal Cortex — The prefrontal cortex is the neural anchor for executive functions: planning, working memory, inhibition, decision-making, and regulation of socially expressed behaviour…
  8. Myelination — Myelin is the biological mechanism that converts effortful, conscious behavior into automatic, effortless habit, and it is built by repetition, not intention.

  10. Flow State — Flow is a measurable neurochemical brain state, distinct from productivity, motivation, or discipline, that produces categorical differences in output quality.
  11. Transient Hypofrontality — Transient hypofrontality is a temporary reduction of activity in the prefrontal cortex.
  12. Time Perception — Retrospective duration can expand when a period leaves many distinctive memories and collapse when routine episodes blur together.
  13. Default Mode Network (DMN) — The default mode network activates during rest and mind-wandering and is suppressed during focused task performance.
  14. Neurochemicals of Wellbeing — Four primary neurochemicals govern subjective wellbeing, and modern life systematically suppresses all four.
  15. Oxytocin — Oxytocin is a neurochemical of bonding, trust, and genuine attachment, released through physical touch, sustained social connection, vulnerability, and shared experience.
  16. Glymphatic System — The glymphatic system is glial-dependent fluid transport along perivascular pathways, one route by which the brain clears metabolic solutes.
  17. Sleep Architecture — Sleep is an actively regulated sequence of NREM and REM states rather than a uniform absence of wakefulness.
  18. Circadian Rhythm — Circadian rhythms are approximately daily patterns coordinated by central and peripheral clocks.
  19. Melatonin — Melatonin is primarily a darkness and circadian-timing signal, not a conventional sedative.
  20. Sleep Optimization Protocol — Sleep optimization treats light, schedule, temperature, caffeine, alcohol, wind-down, and sleep opportunity as one behavioural system.

  22. Nervous System States — Your nervous system is the operating system running everything — not your conscious mind, not your willpower, your autonomic nervous system making split-second decisions…
  23. Adult Neurogenesis — Adult neurogenesis is the production of new neurons after development.
  24. Breathing Protocols — Breathing is an accessible way to change arousal: breath rate and exhalation can alter respiratory chemistry, heart-rate dynamics, and felt activation.
  25. Cognitive Performance Phases — The brain moves through predictable performance phases instead of sustaining maximum output for sixteen hours.
  26. Exercise and the Brain — Exercise is a broadly supported input to brain health, explained through BDNF, vascular change, stress regulation, sleep, and metabolic effects.
  27. Meditation and the Brain — Meditation is structured attention and metacognitive practice.
  28. Theta State — Theta names oscillatory activity in a frequency band, not one unitary mental state.
  29. Executive Control Network — The executive control network is the brain’s sharp, deliberate, effortful mode.
  30. Fasting & Cognitive Performance — Fasting is commonly said to sharpen cognition, and the account here, The Lost Art Of Hunger: Why Three Meals A Day Is Making You Stupid, is a first-person report of…
  31. Interest-Based Attention — The ADHD brain runs an interest-based attention system rather than a broken one.
  32. Lucid Dreaming — A lucid dream is one in which you know you are dreaming.
  33. Nocturnal Clarity — Nocturnal clarity is a late-night surge of clarity, motivation, and creative insight that can appear around bedtime and disappear by morning.
  34. Nutrition and Cognition — What you eat is what your brain is made of and what your brain runs on.
  35. Placebo and Nocebo — Placebo and nocebo responses are physiological effects that positive or negative expectations around an intervention can recruit.
  36. Trauma and the Nervous System — Trauma, in this nervous-system model, is overwhelming experience that remains active in present reactions because the body never completed its threat response.
