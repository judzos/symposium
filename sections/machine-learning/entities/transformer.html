Transformer

The Transformer is a stack of encoders and decoders, introduced in Attention is All You Need (2017). Only self-attention connects positions to one another; the feed-forward network, residual connections and layer normalisation each act on positions independently.

Because positions interact only in the attention layer, the network runs every other operation in parallel across the sequence. This removed the sequential training bottleneck that limited LSTM models.

Why can a Transformer train in parallel across a sequence when an RNN cannot?

Positions interact only inside the attention layer. Everything else — feed-forward, residuals, layer norm — acts on each position independently, so there is no step-by-step dependency to serialise on.

In short

  • Positions interact only in attention, so everything else runs in parallel, removing the sequential bottleneck of recurrent models.
  • The price is cost quadratic in sequence length, which drives long-context research.
  • Removing recurrence removed order, so positional encoding is added back and decoder masking keeps generation causal.
  • Indifference to modality let the Vision Transformer reuse the architecture almost unchanged, with 16×16 patches as tokens.
  • It encodes almost no knowledge about language, which reconciles it with the Bitter Lesson.

What replaced what

Recurrent modelsTransformer
State carried step by stepEvery position attends to every other from layer one
O(n·d²) — linear in sequence length, quadratic in hidden dimensionO(n²·d) — quadratic in sequence length, linear in model dimension
Order is implicit in the recurrenceOrder must be added back as positional encoding
Long-range dependency degrades with distanceDistance costs nothing; sequence length costs quadratically

The complexity row holds the trade1: Transformers gain parallelism at the cost of quadratic scaling with sequence length. That quadratic cost drives long-context research.

What does the Transformer pay for removing the recurrence?

Cost quadratic in sequence length (O(n²·d)) instead of linear. Distance between two positions becomes free, but total length becomes expensive — which is why long-context research exists.

Why does a Transformer need positional encoding at all?

Order was implicit in the recurrence it removed. With every position attending to every other from layer one, nothing in the architecture distinguishes sequence order, so it has to be added back into the embeddings.

The pieces

  • Query / key / value: three learned projections per position. Attention scores each query against every key by dot product, scales by √dk, applies softmax, weights the values and sums them.
  • Multi-head attention: eight independently initialised Q/K/V sets provide eight “representation subspaces.” The layer concatenates them and projects them by WO. In Alammar’s example one head binds “it” to “the animal” while another binds it to “tired.”
  • Positional encoding: sinusoidal vectors added to the input embeddings, because removing recurrence removed all notion of order.
  • Masking: decoder self-attention sets future positions to −inf before the softmax, so generation stays causal.
  • Cross-attention: queries from one source, keys and values from another. This mechanism makes the architecture multimodal.

What does multi-head attention buy over a single attention head?

Several independently initialised Q/K/V sets give several representation subspaces, so different heads can bind the same word to different antecedents — one head tying “it” to “the animal” while another ties it to “tired.”

What does decoder masking do, and why is it necessary?

It sets future positions to −inf before the softmax. Without it, attention over the whole sequence would let a position see the tokens it is supposed to be predicting, so generation would not be causal.

Why it generalised beyond language

The Transformer’s indifference to modality is a more durable contribution than its performance on text1. The Vision Transformer’s architecture is nearly identical to the 2017 original; the only real adaptation is cutting the image into 16×16 patches so that patches can play the role tokens play. Earlier architectures used a separate specialised model per data type, making them one-trick ponies.

What is the single adaptation that turns a Transformer into a Vision Transformer?

Cutting the image into 16×16 patches so patches can play the role tokens play. The architecture is otherwise nearly identical to the 2017 original — which is the evidence that it is indifferent to modality.

The prediction that landed

Understanding LSTM Networks2 closes, in August 2015, by naming attention as the next big step after LSTMs. The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Recurrent Neural Networks3, three months earlier, calls attention “the most interesting recent architectural innovation in neural networks.” Both were written two years before the Transformer paper.

The Transformer complicates The Bitter Lesson4: humans designed its architecture, while Sutton argues that such design plateaus. The two positions fit together because the Transformer encodes almost no knowledge about language. It provides a general method for converting compute into performance, the kind Sutton says survives.

How does the Transformer escape the Bitter Lesson's verdict on human architectural design?

It encodes almost no knowledge about language. What Sutton says plateaus is built-in domain knowledge; a general method for turning compute into performance is the category he says survives.
