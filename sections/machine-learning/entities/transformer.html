Transformer

The architecture introduced in Attention is All You Need (2017): a stack of encoders and decoders in which the only operation connecting positions to each other is self-attention. Everything else — the feed-forward network, the residual connections, the layer normalisation — acts on each position independently.

That single structural fact is the whole advantage. Because positions interact only in the attention layer, the rest of the network runs in parallel across the sequence, which is what removed the sequential training bottleneck that limited LSTM models.

Why can a Transformer train in parallel across a sequence when an RNN cannot?

Positions interact only inside the attention layer. Everything else — feed-forward, residuals, layer norm — acts on each position independently, so there is no step-by-step dependency to serialise on.

What replaced what

Recurrent modelsTransformer
State carried step by stepEvery position attends to every other from layer one
O(n·d²) — linear in sequence length, quadratic in hidden dimensionO(n²·d) — quadratic in sequence length, linear in model dimension
Order is implicit in the recurrenceOrder must be added back as positional encoding
Long-range dependency degrades with distanceDistance costs nothing; sequence length costs quadratically

The complexity row is the honest trade, and amans-ai-journal-primers-transformers spells it out: Transformers win on parallelism and lose quadratically on sequence length. Long-context research exists because of that cell in the table.

What does the Transformer pay for removing the recurrence?

Cost quadratic in sequence length (O(n²·d)) instead of linear. Distance between two positions becomes free, but total length becomes expensive — which is why long-context research exists.

Why does a Transformer need positional encoding at all?

Order was implicit in the recurrence it removed. With every position attending to every other from layer one, nothing in the architecture distinguishes sequence order, so it has to be added back into the embeddings.

The pieces

  • Query / key / value — three learned projections per position. Score by dot product of query against every key, scale by √dk, softmax, weight the values, sum.
  • Multi-head attention — eight independently initialised Q/K/V sets, giving eight “representation subspaces,” concatenated and projected by WO. In Alammar’s example one head binds “it” to “the animal” while another binds it to “tired.”
  • Positional encoding — sinusoidal vectors added to the input embeddings, because removing recurrence removed all notion of order.
  • Masking — decoder self-attention sets future positions to −inf before the softmax, so generation stays causal.
  • Cross-attention — queries from one source, keys and values from another. This is the mechanism that makes the architecture multimodal rather than merely linguistic.

What does multi-head attention buy over a single attention head?

Several independently initialised Q/K/V sets give several representation subspaces, so different heads can bind the same word to different antecedents — one head tying “it” to “the animal” while another ties it to “tired.”

What does decoder masking do, and why is it necessary?

It sets future positions to −inf before the softmax. Without it, attention over the whole sequence would let a position see the tokens it is supposed to be predicting, so generation would not be causal.

Why it generalised beyond language

amans-ai-journal-primers-transformers argues the durable claim is not that Transformers are good at text but that they are indifferent to modality. The Vision Transformer’s architecture is nearly identical to the 2017 original; the only real adaptation is cutting the image into 16×16 patches so that patches can play the role tokens play. Earlier architectures were, in Chadha’s phrase, “one trick ponies” with a separate specialised model per data type.

What is the single adaptation that turns a Transformer into a Vision Transformer?

Cutting the image into 16×16 patches so patches can play the role tokens play. The architecture is otherwise nearly identical to the 2017 original — which is the evidence that it is indifferent to modality.

The prediction that landed

understanding-lstm-networks-colahs-blog closes, in August 2015, by naming attention as the next big step after LSTMs. unreasonable-effectiveness-recurrent-neural-networks, three months earlier, calls attention “the most interesting recent architectural innovation in neural networks.” Both were written two years before the Transformer paper.

Read against bitter-lesson, the Transformer is an awkward case: it is unmistakably a piece of human architectural design, and Sutton’s argument is that such design plateaus. The reconciliation is that it encodes almost no knowledge about language — it is a general method for converting compute into performance, which is exactly the kind of thing Sutton says survives.

How does the Transformer escape the Bitter Lesson's verdict on human architectural design?

It encodes almost no knowledge about language. What Sutton says plateaus is built-in domain knowledge; a general method for turning compute into performance is the category he says survives.
