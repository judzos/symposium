Multilayer Perceptron

A multilayer perceptron is the simplest neural network architecture: it stacks alternating matrix multiplications (plus biases) and term-wise non-linear functions. It does not connect the computations at different positions.

Why the second layer matters

10-neural-networks-nature-code reaches the architecture from its smallest case. A single perceptron multiplies inputs by weights, adds a bias, and thresholds the sum; geometrically, it can learn only one straight decision boundary. That is enough for AND or OR but not XOR, whose true and false cases are not linearly separable. Combining neurons makes the missing composition possible: one can represent OR, another NOT-AND, and their outputs together represent XOR. Hidden layers generalize this move from one boundary to learned combinations of boundaries.

The same construction also explains the bias. Without it, the decision boundary is forced through the origin because zero inputs always yield zero weighted input. A constant input of one with its own learned weight lets the boundary shift. In this geometric reading, weights determine the boundary’s orientation and the bias determines its offset.

What the parts are for

Sanderson derives each component from a requirement:

  • Weights say which pattern a neuron responds to. Drawn as a 28×28 grid, a band of positive weights sums brightness in a region. Because that also fires on blobs, negative weights above and below restrict the response to a narrow edge.
  • The bias says how much is enough. A bias of −10 means the neuron becomes meaningfully active only once the weighted sum exceeds 10.
  • The non-linearity is structurally required. Without it every layer is matrix-multiply-plus-offset, the whole network collapses into one linear function, and expressiveness drops dramatically.

Why do the weights of an edge-detecting neuron carry negatives above and below the band?

A band of positive weights alone sums brightness in a region, which also fires on a solid blob. The negative weights either side make the neuron fire only when the bright region is narrow — an edge.

What does the bias contribute that the weights cannot?

A threshold. Weights say which pattern the neuron responds to; the bias says how strong that match has to be before the neuron becomes meaningfully active.

What happens to a deep network with the non-linearities removed?

Every layer becomes matrix-multiply-plus-offset, so the whole stack collapses into a single linear function. Depth buys nothing without it.

Why ReLU helped deep optimisation

Sigmoid saturates at both extremes, so its derivative can become very small and weaken a gradient propagated through many layers. ReLU retains a constant positive-side derivative, which often makes deep optimisation easier. It is still flat for negative inputs and can produce inactive or “dead” units, so the source’s claim that it “never flattens” is useful shorthand only on the positive side. might-llms-store-facts-deep-learning notes that transformer MLPs often use smoother alternatives such as GELU.

Why did ReLU displace sigmoid, and why did depth make that decisive?

Sigmoid’s derivative becomes small at both extremes. ReLU keeps a nonzero derivative on its positive side, which often preserves stronger gradients through deep stacks—but it is flat on the negative side and can create dead units.

Two readings of the two matrices

might-llms-store-facts-deep-learning reads the two matrix multiplications in different orientations:

MatrixRead asMeaning
W↑ (up-projection)rowseach row is a direction; the dot product asks a question about the input vector
W↓ (down-projection)columnseach column is a direction in embedding space; it is the edit added when its neuron is active

Rows ask, columns answer. In GPT-3, W↑ has just under 50,000 rows, exactly four times the 12,288 embedding dimensions. The designers chose this ratio for hardware friendliness, without a deeper theoretical reason.

Why are the two MLP matrices read in different orientations?

The up-projection’s rows are directions the input is dot-producted against — each row asks a question. The down-projection’s columns are directions in embedding space — each column is the edit added when its neuron fires. Rows ask, columns answer.

”Neurons” means the post-ReLU values

The word “neuron” is overloaded. In a transformer, it refers to a value leaving the ReLU: active when positive and inactive at zero. The familiar dots-and-lines diagram depicts a matrix multiplication followed by a term-wise non-linearity.

Inside a transformer it is the block that does not communicate

transformers-tech-behind-llms-deep-learn places it in alternation with attention: attention lets vectors pass information between positions, then the MLP processes each vector in parallel without sharing information across positions, and its output is added back to what flowed in. Attention moves information among positions; the MLP applies learned feature transformations at each position. The source uses “storage” as an interpretive model rather than a literal separation between memory and computation.

What is the division of labour between attention and the MLP block?

Attention moves information between positions; the MLP processes each position independently and adds its result back. One supplies context, the other supplies the stored capacity.

What it does not do, in either era

  • analyzing-our-neural-network-3blue1brown shows that the digit network’s learned first-layer weights look almost random rather than edge-like, and that it declares random noise to be a 5 with full confidence. The architecture neither uses the adjacency of pixels nor transfers a pattern learned in one region to another, a gap that motivates convolution.

What does an MLP fail to exploit about image data?

That pixels are adjacent. Nothing in the architecture knows about spatial locality, and a pattern learned in one region cannot transfer to another — which is the gap convolution was invented to fill.

  • might-llms-store-facts-deep-learning finds the same disappointment at scale: an MLP neuron rarely represents one clean feature. See superposition.
