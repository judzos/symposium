Self-Attention

Letting every position in a sequence look at every other position and decide, by learned similarity, how much of each to absorb. It is the only operation in a Transformer that connects positions to one another.

flowchart LR
  E[Input embedding] --> P[Project to a query, a key and a value]
  P --> S[Score — this query against every key]
  S --> D[Divide by the square root of d_k, for gradient stability]
  D --> M[Softmax — positive weights summing to 1]
  M --> V[Multiply each value by its weight]
  V --> O[Sum — the output for this position]
  P --> H[Eight heads, eight representation subspaces]
  H --> C[Concatenate and project through W_O]
  O --> C

The computation, in six steps

From illustrated-transformer-jay-alammar-visualizing-machine:

  1. Project each input embedding into a query, a key, and a value (dimension 64 against an embedding dimension of 512 — an architecture choice that keeps multi-head computation roughly constant).
  2. Score each position against every position: dot product of this query with that key.
  3. Divide by 8 — √dk — for gradient stability.
  4. Softmax, so the scores are positive and sum to 1.
  5. Multiply each value vector by its score, drowning out the irrelevant ones.
  6. Sum. That is the output for this position.

In practice all of it is one matrix expression, which is exactly why it parallelises.

What are the three roles a query, a key and a value play?

The query is what this position is looking for, the key is what a position offers for matching, and the value is what actually gets carried across once the match is scored. Query·key decides how much of the value to absorb.

Why divide the attention scores by √d k before the softmax?

For gradient stability. Dot products grow with dimension, and large scores push the softmax into its flat region where gradients vanish.

What does the softmax step do to the attention scores?

Makes them positive and sum to 1, so they act as weights over the value vectors — the irrelevant positions are scaled toward zero and drowned out of the sum.

What it is for

The canonical example: “The animal didn’t cross the street because it was too tired.” What does “it” refer to? Trivial for a reader, not for an algorithm. Self-attention is the mechanism that binds “it” to “animal”, and visualising the top encoder shows the binding happening.

Chadha’s variant in amans-ai-journal-primers-transformers is harder — “The owl spied a squirrel. It tried to grab it with its talons but only got the end of its tail” — and makes the same point: a model attending only to nearby words cannot resolve which “it” is which, while a model connecting every word to every other can work out that the owl grabbed and the squirrel lost part of its tail.

Why does pronoun resolution defeat a model that only attends locally?

The antecedent can be arbitrarily far away, and several candidates may sit nearby. Resolving which “it” is which requires connecting every word to every other, not just to its neighbours.

Multi-head: several opinions at once

Eight independently initialised sets of Q/K/V matrices produce eight outputs, concatenated and projected by a further matrix WO. The gain is representation subspaces: in the same example, one head attends to “the animal” while another attends to “tired,” so the encoding of “it” absorbs both. With a single head, the position’s own value tends to dominate.

What failure mode does a single attention head have?

Its own position’s value tends to dominate the weighted sum, so little context actually gets absorbed. Several heads give several subspaces, letting one bind “it” to “the animal” while another binds it to “tired.”

What it costs

Attention compares every pair of positions, so it is O(n²) in sequence length where recurrence is O(n). Distance within the sequence becomes free — position 1 and position 500 are one hop apart — but total length becomes expensive. Every long-context technique in the field is an attempt to pay less for that n².

What does attention make free, and what does it make expensive?

Distance becomes free — position 1 and position 500 are one hop apart. Total length becomes expensive, because every pair of positions is compared: O(n²) where recurrence was O(n).

Removing recurrence also removes all notion of order, which is why positional encoding has to be added back explicitly.

Cross-attention: the same idea across modalities

Take queries from one source and keys and values from another and the mechanism fuses two different kinds of data. amans-ai-journal-primers-transformers argues this is the deeper significance — not that attention is good at language, but that it is indifferent to what the sequence contains, which is what made one architecture serve text, images and audio.

What single change turns self-attention into cross-attention?

Taking the queries from one source and the keys and values from another. That one substitution lets the mechanism fuse two different kinds of data, which is what made one architecture serve text, images and audio.

Predicted before it arrived

unreasonable-effectiveness-recurrent-neural-networks (May 2015) calls attention “the most interesting recent architectural innovation in neural networks,” discussing soft attention from Neural Turing Machines and the efficiency cost that motivates hard attention. understanding-lstm-networks-colahs-blog (August 2015) closes by naming attention as the next step after LSTMs. Both precede Attention is All You Need by two years.
