The Complete Testosterone And Estrogen Optimization Guide

Hormonal decline is environmental rather than personal — the modern chemical and light environment systematically undermines endocrine function — so the protocol is not biohacking but deliberate compensation for conditions ancestors never had to engineer.

Core Argument

Ixcarus opens with reported declines in mean total testosterone in men, from 605 ng/dL in 1999–2000 to 451 ng/dL by 2015–2016 within a single generation. The piece says levels have declined roughly 1% per year since the late 1980s and puts the prevalence of testosterone deficiency at 20% among adolescent and young adult males. The author compares this with average human height or lung capacity dropping by a quarter in twenty years, when “people would be losing their fucking minds.”

The author locates the causes in the environment: synthetic estrogens in unfiltered tap water from pharmaceutical runoff, phthalates in shampoos and personal care products, microplastics shed by polyester clothing and found in human blood, organs and reproductive tissue, xenoestrogens leaching from plastic food packaging, and BPA absorbed through the skin from receipts. “You are living in a chemical environment that your endocrine system was never designed to handle.”

The piece qualifies its account of female hormones: there is no strong evidence women’s estrogen has seen the same steep generational decline. It links endocrine-disrupting chemicals to menstrual disruption, reduced fertility, miscarriage risk, PCOS, endometriosis, altered puberty timing and hormone-sensitive cancers. The author reports that PCOS affects roughly 10–15% of women of reproductive age and endometriosis approximately 10%.

The author declines to speculate on intent: “I’m not going to get into theories about whether this is deliberate. I don’t know and neither does anyone who says they do with certainty.”

Key Concepts

  • The lock-and-key model of endocrine disruption: Natural testosterone or estrogen is the correctly cut key that fits the lock and produces the right signal. Xenoestrogens are “duplicate keys cut to almost the right shape” that jam the lock, sometimes turning it partially and sometimes blocking the real key. The system then runs on corrupted signals. In men, the author says excess estrogen signalling suppresses testosterone through negative feedback: the body reads estrogen levels as information about how much testosterone to make, so a flood of mimics reduces production.
  • Estrogen dominance: The author says women have a finite number of receptor sites for which xenoestrogens compete. Real estrogen need not rise; estrogen-like signalling can still produce the same downstream effects, including heavier and more painful periods, worse PMS, breast tenderness, difficulty losing weight around hips and thighs, mood instability, and elevated hormone-sensitive cancer risk.
  • The cortisol axis compounding it: The piece says chronic cortisol directly suppresses progesterone, the hormone that balances estrogen in the luteal phase, so sustained stress worsens estrogen dominance through a second mechanism. For men, the author calls chronic cortisol elevation “one of the most direct suppressors of testosterone production” and says “there’s no supplement protocol that overcomes a lifestyle producing sustained psychological stress.”
  • Load reduction as the foundation: The author makes this a prerequisite for adding interventions: “you cannot out-run or even out-supplement a body that’s being chemically undermined from every direction.” Recommendations include reverse-osmosis water filtration at the stated price tiers of countertop 150–400andunder−sink200–600; glass storage instead of plastic because heat accelerates leaching; avoiding synthetic fragrance, where phthalates hide under the label “fragrance”; and prioritising organic produce for thin-skinned fruits and vegetables.
  • The female protocol — cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower, brussels sprouts, cabbage) for indole-3-carbinol supporting hepatic estrogen clearance, 2–3 servings daily, since the liver is the primary organ for estrogen metabolism. Strength training 3–4× weekly with moderate cardio rather than excessive high-intensity cardio, which spikes cortisol enough to suppress reproductive hormones — the reason competitive female athletes often experience cycle disruption. Daily walking as “one of the most underrated interventions.” Adequate dietary fat at 25–30% of calories, because estrogen and progesterone are built from cholesterol and decades of low-fat advice actively undermined production. Omega-3 (2–3 servings fatty fish weekly or 2g EPA+DHA). Magnesium glycinate 300–400mg before bed, required for progesterone production and cortisol regulation. Protein at 0.7–0.9g per pound — “your body literally cannot produce hormones without amino acid building blocks. It’s like trying to build a house with no bricks.” Strict circadian consistency, since the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis depends on consistent light-dark signalling. Reduced alcohol specifically in the luteal phase.
  • The male protocol — heavy compound movements (squat, deadlift, bench, overhead press) produce the strongest acute testosterone response, with 2–3 minute rest periods because 30-second rests keep cortisol elevated and blunt the response. Sleep in full darkness at 65–68°F, since testosterone production peaks in deep sleep between 10pm and 2am and any light in that window suppresses the cascade. Zinc as a direct precursor (oysters highest by a significant margin; 25–30mg daily if not eating red meat 2–3× weekly). Vitamin D as a hormone precursor — 10–30 minutes direct sunlight on significant skin area, or D3 at 3000–5000 IU with K2. Caffeine cut off by noon, since acute morning cortisol supports production while chronic elevation suppresses it. Alcohol reduced to 1–2 drinks weekly, as it suppresses production and accelerates aromatisation of testosterone to estrogen. Phone out of the pocket on precautionary EMF grounds. Pornography addressed as dopamine dysregulation that blunts hormonal response to real stimulation.
  • Evidence honesty: The author presents seed cycling with its limits: “the research is limited… the evidence isn’t strong enough to call this proven,” and recommends it only because the downside risk is essentially zero and the fibre, magnesium and fatty acids help regardless. He also identifies red light therapy, EMF and the onion/luteinizing-hormone claim as supported by limited or animal-model evidence, and discloses a practice he does not personally follow while reporting the research.
  • Balance rather than maximisation: “men need some estrogen. Just not excess. Women need some testosterone. Just not excess.” The author presents the protocol as compensation: ancestors got sunlight, real food, constant movement and darkness at night without engineering them, while “you have to engineer all of it deliberately because modern life stripped it away.”

Notable Passages

“Your body doesn’t know the difference between real hormones and synthetic mimics. It responds to the signal regardless of where the signal came from.”

“They’re selling you the solution to a problem the modern world created.”

“Comfortable modern life is what got you here. And deliberate, slightly inconvenient choices are what get you out.”
