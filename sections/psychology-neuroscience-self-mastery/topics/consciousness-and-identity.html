Consciousness & Identity

“The ‘you’ experiencing this moment cannot be located — it emerges from the system but is not any particular part of it.” — ixcarus

The architecture of self. Who are you beneath the behavior, the thoughts, the social mask? ixcarus approaches this from two directions simultaneously: the neuroscience of consciousness (what the brain is doing) and Jungian depth psychology (what the psyche is doing). The convergence is striking — both suggest the “you” you think you are is the smallest part of what’s actually running.

The Subconscious Operating System

Scale of the Gap

  • The conscious mind processes approximately 40 bits of information per second
  • The subconscious mind (Subconscious-Mind) processes approximately 11,000,000 bits per second
  • This means 97–98% of all decisions, reactions, and behaviors are initiated and completed before conscious awareness
  • The Libet Experiment: brain activity initiating a decision is detectable 7 seconds before the person consciously “decides” — consciousness is not the decision-maker; it is the press secretary

What is the ratio between conscious and subconscious processing?

Roughly 40 bits per second against 11,000,000 — which is why 97–98% of decisions and reactions are initiated and completed before conscious awareness arrives.

Subconscious Programming

  • Patterns installed before age 7 (while the brain is in theta/delta wave state — essentially hypnotic) become the foundation of personality, emotional patterns, and default responses
  • The subconscious runs these programs automatically — without consent, without review
  • Changing surface behavior without addressing the subconscious programming is why most self-improvement fails

Why does programming installed before age 7 have such disproportionate hold?

The brain is in theta/delta states through those years — essentially hypnotic — so what is laid down becomes foundational personality and default response rather than reviewable belief.

Jungian Psychology: The Full Map of Self

Carl Jung produced what ixcarus calls “the most important psychology you will ever read” — a complete map of the psyche that bridges depth psychology to neuroscience.

  • Jungian-Psychology

The Layers of Self

Persona — the social mask. Functional and necessary, but becomes pathological when you can no longer find the face underneath it.

Ego (Ego) — center of conscious awareness, not the whole self. More narrator/press secretary than decision-maker. Neural activity precedes conscious awareness — the ego rationalizes what the deeper system has already done.

Shadow — everything rejected, suppressed, or denied about yourself. It doesn’t disappear; it operates from below consciousness and projects outward: what enrages you most in others is almost always shadow content — the thing you secretly want but have told yourself you cannot have or be.

What does intense, disproportionate irritation at someone usually indicate?

Shadow projection. What is rejected in yourself does not disappear — it operates below consciousness and is projected outward, so the trait that enrages you is often the one you have forbidden yourself.

Personal Unconscious — your private archive of all experiences, emotions, and faded memories. Shapes behavior automatically as “personality.”

Collective Unconscious — shared psychic layer all humans are born with, containing archetypes (Hero, Shadow, Trickster, Great Mother) that appear identically across all cultures with no contact.

Anima/Animus — masculine/feminine aspects carried by each gender. When unintegrated, projected onto romantic partners — explains intense, inexplicable attraction to people who carry what you’ve suppressed in yourself.

In Jungian terms, what explains an intense attraction you cannot account for?

Unintegrated anima or animus projected onto the other person. The pull is toward what you have suppressed in yourself and are seeing carried by someone else.

The Self — the organizing center of the whole psyche; what individuation aims toward.

Individuation

The lifelong process of becoming your actual self by integrating shadow, dissolving performative persona, and reclaiming projected anima/animus. Neurologically maps to neuroplasticity — it is a literal rewiring process.

The Identity Setpoint

The brain is a prediction machine that prefers a familiar hell over an unfamiliar heaven.

  • Identity-Setpoint — the nervous system’s model of “who I am”; behavior must stay consistent with this model
  • When success threatens the identity setpoint, the nervous system course-corrects back toward the familiar — this is Self-Sabotage
  • “Familiar hell over unfamiliar heaven” — the brain optimizes for predictability, not happiness
  • Identity shift is the real intervention: the question is not “how do I stop doing X” but “who is the person who naturally doesn’t do X”
  • Changing behavior without changing identity is impossible — the behavior will keep reverting to match the identity

Why would a prediction machine choose a familiar hell over an unfamiliar heaven?

Because it optimises for predictability, not for happiness. An unfamiliar good state is still unmodelled, and unmodelled reads as risk.

The Hard Problem of Consciousness

ixcarus takes consciousness seriously as an unsolved problem — not dismissed into neuroscience.

  • Hard-Problem-of-Consciousness (David Chalmers): science can explain neural correlates of experience but cannot explain why there is subjective experience — the “what it’s like” — at all
  • No single brain region contains consciousness; it seems to emerge from the whole system, but the mechanism is unknown
  • Theories covered across essays: Global Workspace Theory · Integrated Information Theory · Predictive Processing · Panpsychism · Quantum Consciousness (Penrose-Hameroff)
  • All theories explain correlates of consciousness but not the origin of subjective experience
  • Practical implication: if “you” are not a fixed thing — if consciousness emerges rather than exists as a fixed entity — then you are not stuck as the current version

What exactly is the " hard problem" of consciousness?

Not which brain activity accompanies experience — that is the tractable part — but why there is subjective experience at all. Every theory on offer explains correlates and leaves the origin untouched.

You Are Not Your Thoughts

A recurring ixcarus claim with both Buddhist and neuroscience grounding:

  • Thoughts arise automatically from the subconscious; you did not choose them
  • Identifying with thoughts = identifying with weather patterns you did not create
  • Observer-position: the capacity to watch thoughts without being them is the beginning of agency
  • This connects to the Jungian-Psychology ego distinction: the ego that watches is not the whole self; the part that watches is the Self

What does the observer position actually buy you?

Agency. Thoughts arise automatically from the subconscious and were not chosen; the capacity to watch them without being them is the first point at which anything can be done about them.

The Placebo Effect: Belief Generates Reality

Belief and expectation alone trigger measurable neurological changes — the brain generates physical reality from what it believes is happening.

See Placebo-and-Nocebo for the conditioned treatment loop and its limits.

  • Turin 1996: saline injections produced 30–50% chronic pain reduction — measurable endogenous opioid release from belief alone
  • Mechanisms: expectation → dopamine release in anticipatory pathways → downstream neurochemical cascade
  • Nocebo effect (inverse): negative expectations produce measurable negative physiological outcomes
  • Practical application: belief in your protocol enhances its actual effect — expectation is an active ingredient

What did the 1996 Turin saline study demonstrate?

That expectation alone produces measurable physiology — 30–50% chronic pain reduction from saline, via genuine endogenous opioid release rather than reported feeling.
