Neuroscience & Biology

“Your brain is not broken. It’s doing exactly what it evolved to do — in an environment it was never designed for.” — ixcarus

ixcarus treats biology as the substrate of behavior, identity, motivation, and change. He grounds almost every practical claim in neuroscience, using the mechanism as its justification.

Core Systems

The Dopaminergic System

ixcarus describes dopamine as the brain’s motivational engine, a pursuit/anticipation chemical rather than a pleasure chemical.

  • Dopaminergic-System has four pathways: mesolimbic (reward), mesocortical (focus/executive function), nigrostriatal (habits/movement), tuberoinfundibular (hormonal regulation)
  • Reward Prediction Error: dopamine fires on the gap between expected and actual outcome, not on the reward itself
  • Baseline vs. peaks: artificial dopamine spikes (screens, junk food, pornography) deplete baseline over time → chronic depletion → anhedonia → inability to focus or feel motivated
  • Dopamine shifts from reward → cue once the nigrostriatal pathway encodes a habit

→ See also: Dopamine-and-Reward (full domain page)

The Nervous System (Polyvagal Theory)

ixcarus places the autonomic nervous system at the foundation of mental and physical performance.

  • Polyvagal-Theory describes three states: Ventral Vagal (safe, social, peak performance) · Sympathetic (fight/flight, chronic activation → burnout) · Dorsal Vagal (shutdown/freeze, learned helplessness)
  • The nervous system cannot distinguish modern stressors from physical predators and triggers identical physiological responses to both
  • Neuroception: unconscious threat scanning below awareness that trauma can miscalibrate
  • Co-regulation: nervous systems are contagious; regulated people calm others, while dysregulated people spread dysregulation
  • Fastest regulation interventions: breathwork → cold exposure → movement → social connection

Why do modern stressors produce the same physiology as a predator?

The nervous system has no mechanism for grading the kind of threat. Neuroception scans for danger below awareness and fires the same response, whether the trigger is a predator or an email.

→ Sources: nervous-system-control-center · neuroscience-of-trauma

The HPA Axis & Cortisol Rhythm

When the stress hormone system functions correctly, it produces performance; dysregulation produces burnout.

  • HPA-Axis-and-Cortisol: Hypothalamus → Pituitary (ACTH) → Adrenals (cortisol), with negative feedback loop
  • Cortisol Awakening Response: should peak in first hour of waking, then decline; burnout inverts this
  • Chronic cortisol physically shrinks the hippocampus, impairs the prefrontal cortex, suppresses immunity, disrupts sleep, depresses sex hormones
  • Two burnout types: early-phase (morning anxiety = cortisol too steep) vs. late-phase (exhausted AM, wired PM = inverted rhythm)

What single measurement distinguishes a healthy cortisol rhythm from burnout?

Where the peak sits. Cortisol should peak within the first hour of waking and decline from there; burnout inverts the curve, leaving the peak at the wrong end of the day.

Brain Mechanisms

Neuroplasticity & Myelination

The brain’s ability to rewire itself provides the mechanism behind all habit formation and identity change.

  • Through Neuroplasticity, repeated activation strengthens and eventually automates neural pathways
  • During Myelination, myelin sheaths wrap around heavily-used pathways, making signal transmission faster and more automatic; “practice makes permanent, not perfect”
  • ~1000 repetitions begin to automate a new behavior; myelination follows a biological pace that cannot be rushed
  • Motor engagement (doing) fires circuits that rumination (thinking about doing) cannot activate

Why can't thinking about a skill substitute for practising it?

Motor engagement fires circuits that rumination never reaches. Myelin only wraps pathways that are actually activated, so imagining the rep builds nothing to automate.

→ See also: Behavior-Change, Habit-Formation

Neurogenesis & BDNF

Adults can grow new neurons, primarily in the hippocampus (memory/learning).

  • BDNF, or Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor: exercise-triggered “miracle-gro for the brain”; grows hippocampus, forms new synaptic connections
  • Aerobic exercise provides the most reliable BDNF trigger and measurably improves fluid intelligence
  • Chronic cortisol, sleep deprivation, an ultra-processed diet, and a sedentary lifestyle suppress neurogenesis

What is the most reliable trigger for BDNF, and what does it do?

Aerobic exercise. BDNF grows the hippocampus and forms new synaptic connections — the reason exercise measurably improves fluid intelligence rather than only mood.

→ Source: intelligence-decline

Flow State

Flow is a specific brain state with a measurable neurochemical configuration, rather than a feeling of productivity.

  • Flow-State combines optimal mesocortical dopamine + norepinephrine + anandamide + serotonin
  • Triggers: clear goal + immediate feedback + challenge 4% above current skill level + no interruptions + single-task focus
  • Transient hypofrontality: prefrontal cortex partially deactivates → self-criticism, time perception, and self-monitoring decrease
  • Flow cycle: struggle → release → flow → recovery (recovery is mandatory; pushing through kills the cycle)
  • One 2-hour flow session > 8 hours of distracted shallow work

What is transient hypofrontality, and what does it feel like?

The partial deactivation of the prefrontal cortex during flow. Self-monitoring, self-criticism and the sense of passing time all drop out, because the region generating them has gone quiet.

→ Source: neuroscience-of-flow-state

The Default Mode Network

The default mode network runs during rest to consolidate memory, generate creativity, and process identity.

  • Default-Mode-Network: rest-state brain activity that handles consolidation, creative synthesis, self-referential processing
  • Constant stimulation, task-switching, and phone use suppress it, making boredom neurologically productive
  • The brain consolidates during recovery periods; grinding without recovery degrades output quality

Why is boredom neurologically productive?

It lets the default mode network run, and that is when consolidation, creative synthesis and self-referential processing happen. Constant stimulation suppresses the network that does the work.

Neurochemicals of Wellbeing

ixcarus identifies four primary chemicals that govern subjective wellbeing and argues that modern life suppresses all of them.

  • Neurochemicals-of-Wellbeing
  • Dopamine: pursuit/anticipation; hijacked by social media, pornography, junk food
  • Serotonin: belonging, stability, social calm; suppressed by isolation, comparison culture, poor gut health (90% produced in gut)
  • Endorphins: pain/discomfort response; atrophied because modern life avoids all discomfort; unlocked by intense exercise, laughter, cold exposure
  • Oxytocin: bonding, trust, physical connection; replaced by digital substitutes that don’t activate real pathways (physical touch, eye contact, genuine presence required)

According to ixcarus, modern life suppresses all four by design because chronic mild misery drives consumption.

Why do digital substitutes fail to deliver oxytocin?

Oxytocin is released by physical touch, eye contact and genuine presence. A digital stand-in reproduces the social appearance of those things without triggering the pathway that produces the chemical.

→ Source: four-chemicals-happiness

Health Optimization (Applied Neuroscience)

Sleep

Calling sleep rest misses its role as active biological maintenance.

  • Glymphatic system (Glymphatic-System): brain waste clearance system that activates only during deep sleep; chronic poor sleep → toxic protein accumulation → Alzheimer’s pathway
  • Sleep stages: NREM 1–3 = physical repair + memory consolidation; REM = emotional processing, creativity
  • Chronic sleep deprivation: accelerates cellular aging, shrinks hippocampus, wrecks testosterone, tanks immunity, increases all-cause mortality by 45%
  • Protocol: consistent sleep/wake schedule → dim red lights after sundown → no screens 1h before bed → cool dark room (65–67°F) → delay caffeine 60–90min post-waking

What does the glymphatic system do, and when can it do it?

It clears metabolic waste from the brain, and it only activates during deep sleep — which is why chronic poor sleep allows toxic protein accumulation rather than merely leaving you tired.

→ Source: sleep-protocol

Nutrition & Brain Health

The brain consumes 20% of total energy; food quality directly affects neurological function.

  • Omega-3s (EPA/DHA): structural component of neuronal membranes; anti-inflammatory
  • Creatine: brain energy buffer that improves short-term memory and working memory under stress
  • BDNF-promoting foods: fatty fish, blueberries (BDNF), dark leafy greens, olive oil, dark chocolate
  • IQ killers: ultra-processed foods (neuroinflammation), excessive alcohol (hippocampus shrinkage), trans fats

→ Source: what-neuroscientists-eat

Posture

Posture is a learned motor baseline as well as a visual pose.

  • Repeated forward-head posture recalibrates the proprioceptive map until slouching feels neutral
  • A rounded thorax restricts diaphragmatic range and encourages shallow breathing
  • Momentary correction fails when the motor program and workstation still reinforce the old position
  • Strength, mobility, repeated neutral-position feedback, and eye-level screens retrain the default

By what chain does slouching produce brain fog?

Slouching restricts diaphragmatic range and encourages shallow chest breathing; the source connects that respiratory constraint to lower cognitive clarity.

→ Source: neuroscience-posture

Trauma & The Body

ixcarus locates trauma in the nervous system rather than in memories.

  • Incomplete stress responses (Levine): the body encodes survival programs that continue activating regardless of logic or time
  • The body keeps score (van der Kolk): unprocessed trauma manifests as physical symptoms long after the event
  • Processing requires somatic (body-level) completion; talking about trauma is insufficient without completing the physical stress cycle

Why is talking about trauma insufficient to process it?

Because it is held as an incomplete stress response in the nervous system rather than as a memory. What is unfinished is physical, so completion has to happen at the level of the body.
