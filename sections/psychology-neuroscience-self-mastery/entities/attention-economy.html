Attention Economy

In the attention economy, human attention is the primary commercial commodity. Platforms capture it, and advertisers purchase it. Herbert Simon coined the term; ixcarus uses it as the foundational frame for all technology criticism: you are not the customer; you are the product.

flowchart LR
  U[User attention and behavioural data] --> P[Platform captures it behind a free product]
  P --> A[Advertisers buy eyeballs, attention time, targeting]
  A --> R[Revenue funds the behavioural science teams]
  R --> T[Variable rewards, infinite scroll, autoplay, outrage amplification]
  T --> U
  T --> E[Attention quality eroded, deep work crowded out]
  T --> X[Algorithmic radicalisation]

The Economic Model

Traditional economy: you pay money for a product or service.

Attention economy: you pay attention (and data) for a “free” product. Your attention is then sold to advertisers at a rate determined by how much of it can be captured and how specifically it can be targeted.

The business incentive: every minute you spend on a platform is revenue. Platforms therefore optimize each design decision to maximize time spent rather than your wellbeing, value, or goals.

Why does "you are the product" follow from the platform being free?

Because the transaction still has to close. If money is not coming from you, what is being sold is your attention and data — so you are what is delivered to the paying party, not what is served.

The model before platforms

The structure is older than the internet. Benjamin Day’s 1833 penny press priced the newspaper cheaply, used sensational stories to gather readers, and charged advertisers for access to them. Commercial television later used programmes to hold an audience between advertisements. Digital platforms add continuous measurement and targeting, but preserve the same three roles: subsidised content gathers attention, a publisher packages it, and an advertiser pays to reach it.

Herbert Simon’s scarcity argument explains why this works. As available information expands, the capacity to process it does not. The supply of content can approach infinity while waking attention remains finite, making selection rather than access the binding constraint.

The Behavioral Engineer’s Toolkit

Platforms employ behavioral scientists with an explicit mandate to maximize engagement. They use:

  • Variable reward schedules (Intermittent-Reinforcement): the most addictive reward pattern in behavioral science; social media feeds are designed around it
  • Social validation loops: likes, comments, and follower counts activate social status instincts
  • Loss aversion: FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out); the fear of what might be happening without you
  • Outrage amplification: negative emotional content drives higher engagement metrics; algorithms reward and amplify divisive content
  • Infinite scroll: removes natural stopping points; the book ends, the TV show credits roll, the feed never stops
  • Autoplay: the next video loads before you choose, eliminating the decision to continue

What do infinite scroll and autoplay remove, specifically?

The moment of decision. A book ends and credits roll — those are natural stopping points where continuing has to be chosen. Removing them means the default is always to keep going.

The Commodity Being Sold

Advertisers buy:

  1. Eyeballs: raw exposure, measured by the number of people who see an ad
  2. Attention time: minutes of sustained attention, which are more valuable than impressions
  3. Behavioral data: what you click, how long you pause, and what you search, enabling increasingly targeted manipulation
  4. Emotional state manipulation: research has shown that platforms can change users’ emotional states, making them more or less susceptible to purchases

From observation to vulnerability targeting

Captured attention produces behavioral data, and that data increases the value of the next unit of attention. Searches, location, purchases, dwell time and activity patterns become a profile used to predict what will hold someone and when they may be susceptible. In the primary work, Target’s inference of pregnancy from shopping patterns illustrates prediction of an undisclosed condition; ads for payday loans aimed at financially distressed users illustrate the next step, applying pressure to an inferred vulnerability.

The loop reinforces itself: more time on a platform creates more observations, which improve targeting and hold attention longer. Cambridge Analytica extended the same logic from consumption to political behavior by using personal data to select messages intended to trigger rather than inform.

Why is attention time worth more to an advertiser than an impression?

An impression is exposure; sustained attention is engagement that can be measured, targeted and priced. The behavioural data it generates is what makes the next placement more precise.

What Is Being Extracted

Time: 47 videos watched, 3 remembered; the rest was time spent. At the social scale, billions of human hours per day are redirected toward attention commodity production.

Attention quality: the focused, sustained attention required for deep-work, learning, and creativity is structurally incompatible with attention economy participation. The two compete for the same cognitive resource.

What does "47 videos watched, 3 remembered" measure?

The extraction rate. Forty-four of those videos left nothing behind — the time was converted into an attention commodity without returning anything to the person who spent it.

Political epistemics: the attention economy provides the infrastructure for algorithmic radicalization (Algorithmic-Radicalization). Outrage-optimized content moves users toward more extreme positions over time because extreme positions produce more engagement.

The Asymmetry

Platforms and users enter the attention economy with an asymmetry:

  • Platforms: billion-dollar budgets, teams of behavioral scientists, 24/7 optimization, years of accumulated behavioral data
  • User: a human, with depleted willpower by evening, a dopamine system exploitable by variable reward, and no training in the mechanics being used against them

Relying on willpower against the attention economy resembles fighting a casino’s slot machines through individual self-discipline. Individual discipline cannot overcome the casino’s advantage; you have to change the structural conditions.

Why is willpower the wrong instrument against the attention economy?

The match is not fair. Billion-dollar budgets, behavioural science teams and years of data are optimising against one depleted person with no training in the mechanics. The winning move is changing the structural conditions, not out-resisting the machine.

Deliberate attention as the countermodel

The primary work proposes control over attention allocation rather than complete technological withdrawal. It recommends five interventions for moving attention from reactive to deliberate:

  • remove the morning cue by charging the phone outside the bedroom;
  • measure screen time rather than estimating it;
  • batch consumption into defined windows;
  • reserve high-signal morning hours for deep-work; and
  • declare an intention and exit condition before opening a platform.

These interventions target different points in the capture loop. Cue removal prevents initiation, batching weakens variable-reward checking, protected hours give scarce attention to a chosen object, and an explicit intention restores the stopping decision that infinite scroll and autoplay remove.
